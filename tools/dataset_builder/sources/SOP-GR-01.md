## 1. Purpose

A well-handled complaint often turns an unhappy guest into a loyal one. This SOP gives every guest-facing colleague the same approach, the same limits and the same escalation path.

## 2. Handling complaints

### 2.1 The CARE model

Complaints must be handled using the CARE model: Calm, Acknowledge, Resolve, Echo back.

### 2.2 In practice

Stay calm and listen without interrupting; acknowledge the guest's feelings; agree a resolution; then echo back what was agreed so that the guest leaves the conversation certain of the next step.

### 2.3 Logging

Every complaint must be logged in the Guest Incident Log within 30 minutes, including the resolution offered.

## 3. Service recovery authority

### 3.1 Guest Relations and Front Office

Guest Relations Executives and Front Office Associates may offer service recovery up to USD 50 per stay; higher amounts require Duty Manager approval.

### 3.2 Duty Manager

The Duty Manager may approve service recovery up to USD 300 per stay; above this amount, the Hotel Manager must approve.

### 3.3 Typical recovery options

| Situation | Typical recovery |
|---|---|
| Delayed room readiness | Coffee and pastry voucher or late check-out |
| Noise disturbance | Room move and a breakfast credit |
| Billing error | Immediate correction and a written apology |
| Room quality issue | Room move and an amenity |

## 4. Assessment

### 4.1 Role-play

Guest Relations Executives must demonstrate complaint handling in a role-play covering a billing dispute and a room-quality complaint.

## 5. Escalation

### 5.1 Sensitive complaints

Complaints involving safety, discrimination or data privacy must be escalated to the Duty Manager immediately, regardless of value.
