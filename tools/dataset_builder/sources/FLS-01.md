## 1. Orientation

### 1.1 Overview

Hotels and serviced apartments are occupied day and night by people unfamiliar with the building. Every Aurelle colleague plays a part in keeping them safe in an emergency.

### 1.2 First-day orientation

Every new employee must attend a fire and life safety orientation on their first day.

### 1.3 Knowing your area

Employees must know the location of the fire assembly point and the two nearest fire exits for their work area.

## 2. Alarm response

### 2.1 RACE procedure

On hearing the fire alarm, employees must follow the RACE procedure (Rescue, Alarm, Contain, Evacuate) and must not use the lifts.

### 2.2 Alarm types

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Intermittent tone | Alert: stand by for instructions |
| Continuous tone | Evacuate the building |
| Voice message | Instructions from the fire command centre |

## 3. Drills and practical training

### 3.1 Drill frequency

All employees must participate in a fire drill every six months.

### 3.2 Drill records

Attendance at drills is recorded by the Security team and visible in the employee's training record.

### 3.3 Night drills

At least one drill each year takes place during the night shift.

### 3.4 Extinguisher practical

Supervisory and engineering staff must demonstrate correct use of a fire extinguisher using the PASS technique during practical training.

## 4. Evacuation roles

### 4.1 Floor wardens

Floor wardens are nominated in every department and wear a high-visibility vest during evacuations.

### 4.2 Duty Manager

The Duty Manager must carry the evacuation master key and the in-house guest list during every shift and lead the evacuation roll call.

## 5. Guests requiring assistance

### 5.1 Assistance list

Front Office must print the list of guests requiring evacuation assistance at the start of every shift.

### 5.2 Refuge areas

Each floor has a protected refuge area next to the fire staircase, marked with green signage.
